# Passive Humidity-Buffering Fry Packaging

## Concept Summary
Develop a food-safe, passive fry packaging system that preserves crispiness during delivery by controlling humidity rather than increasing ventilation. The packaging integrates a sealed humidity-buffering layer composed of sodium bicarbonate embedded within a porous natural fiber matrix (such as cellulose or bagasse), bound with an inert plant-based binder. As hot fries release steam, moisture is redirected away from the food and interacts with the bicarbonate-fiber layer, reducing condensation and preventing stagnant humid air while maintaining heat. The system requires no electronics, no changes to cooking methods, and is compatible with existing packaging lines. Designed to be low-cost, scalable, and biodegradable, the solution targets the root cause of soggy fries and can extend to other fried foods in delivery environments.

## The Problem
The "Soggy Fry" problem is the primary challenge in the food delivery industry. Traditional packaging forces a trade-off:
1. **Sealed/Closed Packaging:** Retains heat but traps steam, leading to rapid condensation and a loss of crispiness (sogginess).
2. **Vented Packaging:** Allows steam to escape but causes rapid heat loss, resulting in cold fries.

This project introduces a "third way": **Humidity Buffering**. By actively managing the vapor phase within the package, we can prevent condensation while keeping the container closed to retain thermal energy.
